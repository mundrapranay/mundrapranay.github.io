      About

      I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Yale University (expected 2028) and an Applied Researcher Intern at eBay. At Yale I am part of the WildAlgs Lab, advised by Professor Quanquan Liu. My research sits at the intersection of algorithms, differential privacy, distributed and high-performance systems, and machine learning.

      I have developed distributed differentially private graph algorithms with substantially improved empirical accuracy, learning-augmented methods for private online analytics, scalable semantic-search systems, neural architectures for mathematical reasoning, and GPU-accelerated scientific software. My work has appeared at venues including VLDB, ICDE, and SIGMOD, with research experience across Yale, MIT CSAIL, Caltech, the University of Rochester, and the University of Washington.

      Research Themes

      Five areas run through my work.

      Differential Privacy

      Algorithms with provable privacy guarantees that stay accurate at scale — local edge-DP graph analytics and learning-augmented private online analytics.

      Distributed Computing

      Parallel and distributed algorithms, communication-aware evaluation, and systems that process billion-edge graphs efficiently.

      ML Systems

      Distributed graph learning and GNN training infrastructure, sparse representations, and performance-oriented model training.

      Data Mining

      Semantic set search, coreset selection, and approximate query answering over large, noisy, and incomplete data.

      Database Systems

      Query models, schema and dependency discovery, and high-performance data access over large text and graph corpora.
